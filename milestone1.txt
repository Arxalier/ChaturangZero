1. The Board and Setup
The Board (Ashtapada): The game is played on an 8x8 uncheckered grid. While historical boards featured specific geometric markings on certain squares, these are purely decorative and have no impact on gameplay or piece movement.
Starting Positions: Each player controls an army of 16 pieces. The pieces mirror each other symmetrically.
King and Minister Placement: The King (Rāja) and Minister (Mantri) occupy the two central files (d and e). Crucially, the Kings must face each other on the same file, and the Ministers must face each other on the same file (e.g., White King on e1, Black King on e8; White Minister on d1, Black Minister on d8).

3. Pawn Promotion
When a Foot Soldier (Padāti) advances to the furthest rank on the opposite side of the board (the 8th rank for White, the 1st rank for Black), it promotes.
The Rule: The Foot Soldier promotes exclusively and automatically to a Minister (Mantri).
Unlike modern chess, there is no choice in promotion, and it is not restricted by previously captured pieces.
4. Terminal States (Game Over Conditions)
The game ends immediately when any of the following three conditions are met. These represent your reward triggers for the reinforcement learning environment:
Checkmate (+1 Win / -1 Loss): The opponent's King is under attack (in check) and has no legal moves to escape, block, or capture the attacking piece.
Baring the King (+1 Win / -1 Loss): A player captures the very last piece of the opponent's army, leaving their King entirely alone on the board. The player who successfully bares the opponent's King instantly wins.
Stalemate (+1 Win / -1 Loss): A player's King is not currently in check, but that player has no legal moves available for any piece on the board. In a departure from modern chess, the player who is stalemated wins the game. (not a draw)
